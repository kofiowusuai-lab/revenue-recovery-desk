## Key entity issue
The contract must identify a real legal contracting party. If the limited company is not yet incorporated or not yet the entity you want to use, do **not** sign merely as “FlowAudit” with no legal person behind it.

Interim decision: use the founder as the contracting party, trading as FlowAudit, until a corporate entity is in place. Use this as the default drafting basis:

- **Interim structure:** `[Founder Legal Name] trading as FlowAudit`.

Other solicitor-approved structures to revisit later:

- **Existing company:** `[Legal Company Name] Ltd trading as FlowAudit`, with company number and registered office.
- **Sole trader / founder pre-incorporation:** `[Founder Legal Name] trading as FlowAudit` until the company exists. This may create personal liability and should be accepted consciously.
- **Pre-incorporation transfer:** Include an assignment/novation clause allowing the agreement to transfer to a future company or group entity, with client consent if required.
- **New company before first paid client:** safest clean route if you want the company, not an individual, to contract from day one.

Companies Act 2006 section 51 makes pre-incorporation contracts a real risk: a person purporting to contract for a company before it exists can become personally liable unless the contract says otherwise and is structured properly.

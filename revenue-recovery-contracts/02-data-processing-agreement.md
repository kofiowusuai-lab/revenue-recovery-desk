# Revenue Recovery Desk Data Processing Agreement

**Solicitor-review draft — UK GDPR Article 28 focused**

This DPA forms part of the Master Services Agreement between the Provider and Client.

## 1. Roles
1.1 For Client customer/debtor personal data processed to provide revenue recovery workflows, the Client is the controller and the Provider is the processor.

1.2 The Provider may be an independent controller for its own account management, billing, fraud prevention, security, legal, audit, support and business administration records.

## 2. Processing details
**Subject matter:** revenue recovery, payment reminders, invoice analysis, reporting and related support.  
**Duration:** term of the Agreement plus retention/deletion period.  
**Nature/purpose:** hosting, accessing, analysing, classifying, drafting, sending approved communications, logging, reporting, support and security.  
**Data subjects:** Client personnel, Client customers/debtors, debtor contacts, authorised users, support contacts.  
**Personal data:** names, roles, business contact details, invoice references, balances, due dates, payment status, communications, notes, approval records, system identifiers, audit logs.  
**Sensitive data:** not expected or authorised unless expressly agreed in writing.

## 3. Documented instructions
3.1 The Provider will process Client personal data only on documented Client instructions, including the Agreement, Order Form, Recovery Authority, approved workflows and authorised-contact instructions.

3.2 The Provider may refuse or suspend instructions that appear unlawful, unsafe, outside scope or technically insecure.

## 4. Confidentiality
The Provider will ensure persons authorised to process personal data are bound by confidentiality obligations.

## 5. Security measures
The Provider will maintain appropriate technical and organisational measures, including where applicable:
- per-client logical isolation;
- least-privilege access;
- OAuth/secure vault credential handling;
- encryption in transit and appropriate encryption/storage protection at rest;
- audit logs;
- approval gates and do-not-contact controls;
- secure offboarding and credential destruction;
- access revocation for staff/contractors;
- incident response procedures.

## 6. Subprocessors
6.1 The Client gives general authorisation for the Provider to use subprocessors needed to provide the Service.

6.2 Current subprocessor categories include hosting/application hosting, database/storage, payment processing, email sending/receiving, physical letter delivery where enabled, isolated runtime/desktop infrastructure, AI/LLM providers, and the Client’s own connected systems.

6.3 The Provider will impose data-protection obligations on subprocessors that are materially equivalent to this DPA.

6.4 The Provider will notify the Client of material subprocessor changes by updating the policy page or written notice. The Client may object on reasonable data-protection grounds.

## 7. International transfers
If personal data is transferred outside the UK/EEA in a way requiring safeguards, the Provider will use appropriate transfer mechanisms such as adequacy regulations, UK IDTA/Addendum, SCCs, or another lawful mechanism.

## 8. Data subject requests
The Provider will provide reasonable assistance to the Client for data subject requests where the request relates to Client-controlled data and the Provider can access the relevant data.

## 9. Personal data breach
The Provider will notify the Client without undue delay after becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting Client-controlled personal data. Notice will include available details about the nature of the incident, affected data, likely consequences and mitigation steps.

## 10. DPIAs and consultations
The Provider will provide reasonable information to help the Client complete DPIAs or regulator consultations where required and relevant to the Service.

## 11. Deletion/return
On termination or verified offboarding, the Provider will delete, return, anonymise or archive personal data according to the Agreement and retention schedule. Live credentials will be revoked/destroyed where technically possible. Limited records may be retained for legal, accounting, security, audit and dispute-resolution purposes.

## 12. Audit and information
The Provider will make available reasonable information needed to demonstrate compliance with this DPA. Audits must be reasonable, on notice, during business hours, subject to confidentiality, and must not compromise other clients, security or secrets.

## 13. Client obligations
The Client is responsible for lawful basis for sharing and processing debtor/customer data; privacy notices to customers/debtors where required; data accuracy; honouring suppression/do-not-contact and dispute instructions; avoiding upload of sensitive, consumer-credit or regulated data unless expressly agreed; and ensuring connected systems and accounts are lawfully authorised.
